IoT Monitoring

Turning sensor readings into facilities insight

One part of the internship involved reviewing data generated by deployed IoT sensors and using the readings to identify patterns that could have practical facilities-management implications.

02

Recorded measurements were reviewed for unusual values, changes, and recurring patterns.

03

Interpret

Sensor behaviour was considered in the context of the monitored facility rather than treating individual readings in isolation.

04

Recommend

Observations were translated into practical recommendations that could improve the monitored environment.

Technical Activity

Identifying a CO₂ pattern through sensor data

During the internship, I reviewed environmental sensor data from a monitored facility and identified a recurring CO₂ spike pattern. The observation was used to recommend a change to the surrounding facility arrangement.

From measurement to recommendation

Rather than treating the sensor readings as isolated numbers, I looked at the pattern of CO₂ measurements and considered what activity or physical arrangement could explain the observed behaviour. The resulting recommendation was to move a drop-off point away from an entrance area, reducing the likelihood that vehicle activity and accumulated emissions would contribute to the observed CO₂ concentration.

This experience demonstrated how IoT monitoring can be useful not only for collecting data, but also for supporting practical decisions about a facility.

Project Leadership

Leading the T3G sustainability proposal

As part of the internship assessment, I served as Project Leader for T3G (Think Green, Go Green), a sustainability campaign proposal developed by a three-person intern team.

I owned the proposal structure from end to end, including the executive summary, action plan, timeline, requirements, and presentation. I also assigned responsibilities across a team with different academic and professional backgrounds and kept the proposed project within its intended budget.

The proposal was presented to an external university stakeholder on behalf of MCfm. During the presentation, concerns were raised about the original one-month timeline and contractual requirements surrounding the proposed campaign. I responded by proposing that the campaign timeline be extended to six months, giving the organisations more time to prepare.

The revised direction was accepted by MCfm at the meeting, after which the stakeholder requested a formal letter of intent for further review.
